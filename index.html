Part B: Answer in One Sentence (Flashcards) 15 Items
Sentence 01
Q1. Define male sterility.
Answer: Male sterility is the condition where a plant fails to produce viable, functional pollen grains while its female organs (pistil) remain normal and fertile.
Sentence 02
Q2. Why is male sterility useful in cotton?
Answer: It completely eliminates the labor-intensive and expensive requirement of hand emasculation, making commercial hybrid seed production cheaper and genetically pure.
Sentence 03
Q3. Name two types of male sterility.
Answer: Genetic Male Sterility (GMS) and Cytoplasmic Male Sterility (CMS) (or Cytoplasmic-Genetic Male Sterility / CGMS).
Sentence 04
Q4. What is Rf?
Answer: Rf is a dominant nuclear "Restorer of Fertility" gene capable of suppressing the effect of sterile cytoplasm and restoring pollen fertility in hybrid progeny.
Sentence 05
Q5. What are gametocides?
Answer: Gametocides (Chemical Hybridizing Agents) are chemical compounds sprayed to induce selective, temporary male sterility without damaging female fertility.
Sentence 06
Q6. What is the function of the B line?
Answer: The B line (maintainer line) has normal cytoplasm with non-restorer genes and is crossed with the male-sterile A line to multiply and perpetuate the A line.
Sentence 07
Q7. How is a GMS line maintained?
Answer: By crossing homozygous sterile female plants (ms ms) with heterozygous fertile maintainer plants (Ms ms), yielding a 1:1 ratio of sterile to fertile plants.
Sentence 08
Q8. What is roguing?
Answer: Roguing is the systematic manual identification and uprooting of off-type, undesirable, or fertile pollen-shedding plants from a seed multiplication plot.
Sentence 09
Q9. What is emasculation?
Answer: Emasculation is the surgical removal of immature anthers or the staminal column from a bisexual flower bud before anther dehiscence.
Sentence 10
Q10. Why are pollinated flowers bagged?
Answer: Bagging shields the receptive stigma from foreign, stray pollen carried by wind or insect vectors, ensuring absolute genetic hybrid purity.
Sentence 11
Q11. What is heterosis?
Answer: Heterosis (hybrid vigour) is the phenotypic superiority, growth vigour, and productivity displayed by an F₁ hybrid compared to both of its inbred parents.
Sentence 12
Q12. What is anthesis?
Answer: Anthesis is the morphological opening of the flower bud, marking the period when anthers dehisce and the stigma becomes receptive for pollination.
Sentence 13
Q13. Which Bt genes are present in BG-II?
Answer: Bollgard-II (BG-II) cotton contains two insecticidal crystal endotoxin genes: Cry1Ac and Cry2Ab.
Sentence 14
Q14. State one limitation of GMS.
Answer: In the female parent rows, 50% of the plants segregate as fertile heterozygotes (Ms ms) and must be manually rogued before they shed pollen.
Sentence 15
Q15. What is the tapetum?
Answer: The tapetum is the innermost nutritive cellular layer of the anther microsporangium wall that provides nutrition to developing microspores.

Part D: OJT Field Application Scenarios 10 Field Items
OJT Practical 01
Q1. Visual Field Diagnosis: How can you identify a male-sterile cotton flower in the experimental field at Mamurabad during anthesis?
Field Diagnosis: Sterile anthers appear pale, shriveled, thin, and translucent; they do not burst or shed powdery yellow pollen onto the petals or stigma when shaken.
OJT Practical 02
Q2. Microchemical Staining: What test did you observe in the field laboratory to verify pollen sterility under a microscope?
Staining Protocol: Staining with 1% I₂-KI (Lugol's iodine) or 1% acetocarmine. Fertile pollen grains with dense starch contents stain uniform dark brown/black; sterile grains remain unstained, shrunken, and empty.
OJT Practical 03
Q3. Doak Method vs. GMS: Why does using a GMS female line save up to 80% labor over the conventional Doak method?
Labor Economics: In the Doak method, laborers must surgically slice the bracts and strip the monadelphous staminal column from unopened buds. In GMS plots, emasculation is done by genetics, so workers only need to dust pollen.
OJT Practical 04
Q4. Roguing Timing in GMS: At what exact growth phase and daily timing must roguing of fertile plants be done in GMS female lines?
Operational Protocol: Daily at the crack of dawn (7:00 AM – 8:30 AM) right at initial flower flush. The 50% heterozygous fertile plants (shedding yellow pollen) must be pulled out by the roots before insects can spread their pollen.
OJT Practical 05
Q5. Planting Ratio: What row ratio of Female (A line) to Male (R line) is planted in the crossing plots?
Plot Layout: Typically 4:1 or 5:1 (4 to 5 female rows per 1 male pollinator border row). This provides convenient walking corridors for dusting male pollen onto female flowers.
OJT Practical 06
Q6. Morphological Markers: Why do cotton breeders link marker genes (like red stem or okra leaf) to the male sterility gene?
Genetic Marker Role: Morphological markers allow farm workers to identify and rogue fertile segregants during the early seedling stage, rather than having to wait for flower anthesis.
OJT Practical 07
Q7. Isolation Distance: What isolation distance is mandatory for certified hybrid cotton seed production?
Certification Norm: A minimum physical separation of 30 to 50 meters from any other cotton field to eliminate insect-mediated pollen drift.
OJT Practical 08
Q8. EGMS Distinction: How do Thermosensitive (TGMS) and Photoperiod-sensitive (PGMS) male sterilities work?
EGMS Dynamics: TGMS plants become sterile above high threshold temperatures (>32°C) and revert to self-fertile under cooler regimes (<28°C), enabling self-multiplication without needing a maintainer line.
OJT Practical 09
Q9. Harknessii CMS Breakdown: Why did G. harknessii CMS lines run into issues in Khandesh?
Agronomic Pitfall: High summer temperatures in northern Maharashtra often led to sterility breakdown, poor fertility restoration in elite cultivars, and inadequate boll set.
OJT Practical 10
Q10. Boll Setting Calculation: If 100 male-sterile flowers are hand-pollinated and 42 develop into harvestable bolls, calculate the boll-setting success percentage.
Calculation:
Boll Setting (%) = (Total Harvestable Bolls / Total Crossed Flowers) × 100
Boll Setting (%) = (42 / 100) × 100 = 42%.
